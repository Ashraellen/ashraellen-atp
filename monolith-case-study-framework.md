# MONOLITH Case Study Framework

MONOLITH serves as evidence that ATP emerged from sustained multilingual literary production rather than from a hypothetical workflow.

This framework defines how a public example may be constructed without placing substantial literary text or private operational history into the repository.

## Public case-study unit

A case study should contain only the minimum excerpt needed to demonstrate one methodological problem:

1. **Context** — volume, source language, target language, and unit type.
2. **Literary problem** — what would be lost by naive literal translation or generic smoothing.
3. **Applicable constraints** — only rules needed to understand the example.
4. **Short source excerpt** — author-approved and publication-safe.
5. **Candidate rendering** — one or more bounded examples where useful.
6. **Validation findings** — terminology, voice, semantics, structure, dialogue, continuity.
7. **Human decision** — accepted wording or recorded rejection.
8. **State effect** — whether the decision created a reusable future constraint.

## Rights rule

A case study does not license MONOLITH as a whole. Any literary excerpt must carry an explicit rights statement separate from the methodology/template license.

## Provenance rule

A public case study may state that production used durable state artifacts such as glossary, index, validation, and continuity records. It should not expose private recovery captures, private chat archives, or internal system instructions merely to prove that they exist.

## Selection criteria

Prefer examples demonstrating locked fictional terminology, ambiguity preservation, dialogue-temperature preservation, intentional resistance to smoothing, conceptual capitalization, cross-chapter continuity, or literal equivalence rejected in favor of functional equivalence.

## Public-safety gate

Before inclusion confirm excerpt authorization, minimal length, no accidental unpublished plot exposure, no private operational/personal data, accurate attribution of AI and human roles, and explicit licensing.

Actual literary excerpt selection remains a separate author-approval gate.
