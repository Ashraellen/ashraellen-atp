# Synthetic Demonstrator

This example was written specifically for protocol demonstration. It is not an excerpt from MONOLITH.

## Context

Source language: Russian  
Target language: English  
Problem: semantic pressure, repetition, and deliberate conceptual use of the word “question”.

## Source

> Система не сказала ему «нет». Она просто перестала считать его вопрос вопросом.

## Governing constraints

- Do not explain the system's motive.
- Preserve the repetition of `вопрос / вопросом` as functional, not accidental.
- Do not replace the second occurrence with a synonym merely for stylistic variety.
- Preserve the bureaucratic coldness of the action.

## Candidate A

> The system did not tell him no. It simply stopped treating his inquiry as a valid question.

### Validation

Semantic meaning is broadly present, but `inquiry` and `valid question` add interpretation. The target explains the mechanism and reduces the source's recursive pressure.

Outcome: `REJECTED`.

## Candidate B

> The system did not tell him “no.” It simply stopped considering his question a question.

### Validation

- Source integrity: PASS
- Semantic fidelity: PASS
- Repetition: PASS
- Bureaucratic coldness: PASS
- Added explanation: none
- Authorship continuity: PASS for demonstration constraints

## Human decision

Candidate B: `ACCEPTED`.

## State effect

No global glossary entry is required, but the example demonstrates a reusable principle:

> Apparent stylistic redundancy may be a semantic mechanism. Do not remove repetition until its function has been evaluated.

## Why this matters

A generic fluency optimizer is likely to prefer Candidate A because it avoids repetition and sounds more idiomatic. ATP prefers Candidate B because the repeated category `question → question` carries the literary action itself: institutional power operates by redefining what qualifies as a thing.

This is the difference between polishing a sentence and preserving the intelligence that generated it.
