# Human–AI Responsibility Model

## Foundational principle

> **Success is measured by how well the system prevents the human from losing themselves.**

The purpose of ATP is not to maximize autonomous generation. It is to preserve authorial identity while using AI to extend human reach across languages, long-form continuity, and production scale.

The central question is not *How much of the work can AI do instead of the author?* It is *How much complexity can AI absorb without displacing the author from the work?*

## Assistance vs authority

AI may increase speed, search space, memory support, comparison capacity, consistency checking, and linguistic variation. It does not acquire authority over canon, meaning, voice, ambiguity, or publication merely because it contributed language.

The human remains the final responsible agent.

### Human authority

The human author or designated editor owns responsibility for:

- defining source canon;
- deciding constrained or intentionally ambiguous meaning;
- determining acceptable literary voice;
- approving names, terminology, metaphors, rhythm, and structural departures;
- deciding when literal equivalence is inferior to functional equivalence;
- rejecting fluent but false or flattening output;
- authorizing exceptions;
- accepting final text for publication.

Human authority is an active control layer throughout the process, not a ceremonial approval step at the end.

### AI assistance

AI may assist with candidate transcreations, alternative renderings, terminology-drift detection, semantic-risk detection, dialogue checks, continuity reconstruction from authorized state, conflicts between local phrasing and global constraints, and repetitive verification.

AI suggestions remain proposals until accepted by the human authority layer.

## Non-substitution principle

A system that produces fluent target-language prose while gradually replacing the author's rhythm, ambiguity, humour, pressure, conceptual vocabulary, or aesthetic resistance has failed, even if its output appears polished.

Efficiency is subordinate to identity preservation.

## Authorship continuity test

A transcreated passage should remain recognizably governed by the same authorial intelligence as the source. The target need not reproduce every source sentence structurally, but it should preserve governing decisions closely enough that the author recognizes their own work rather than a fluent interpretation produced on their behalf.

Warning signs include deliberate roughness being polished into generic elegance, ambiguity resolved for convenience, philosophical density simplified into explanation, humour normalized, recurring concepts replaced by near-synonyms, dialogue voices converging, and unusual rhythm being corrected simply because it is unusual.

## Asymmetry of responsibility

Contribution volume does not determine authority. AI may generate hundreds of candidate sentences and still possess zero canonical authority. A human may make one decisive rejection and preserve the identity of an entire passage.

## Controlled disagreement

AI may identify a risk, explain a conflict with existing constraints, and propose alternatives. The human decides whether an existing rule, an exception, or candidate text becomes authoritative. Decisions that affect future work are recorded in project state.

## Memory without authorship

Externalized project memory reduces context loss across sessions and tools. It is a record of previously authorized decisions, not a second author. A glossary constrains future language because its entries derive authority from prior human approval; a bootstrap restores context but does not redefine canon; a validation checklist may flag deviation but cannot decide whether deviation is artistically correct.

## Preservation over imitation

Style imitation asks: *Can the system write something that sounds like this author?*

ATP asks: *Can the system help this author move their own work into another language without becoming somebody else in the process?*

## Failure criterion

The protocol has failed when the output is technically competent but the author must surrender identity in order to obtain scale.

**The system should increase the author's reach without decreasing the author's presence.**

**Human-authored. Human-directed. AI-assisted. State-controlled. Canonically approved.**
